\newcommand{\eqa}[1]{\begin{align} #1 \end{align}}$***Recap from Tuesday:*** A closed, non-degenerate 2-form on a smooth manifold $M$ is called a *symplectic structure*.
$~$
Given $\, (M, \omega), \ {}$ define ${\ }^\flat: \cXM \to \dcXM\,{}$ and ${\ }^\sharp: \dcXM \to \cXM\,{}$ by
$$
X^\flat := \iota_X \omega \sands \iota_{\beta^\sharp} \omega = \beta.
$$

Given $\, h \in C^\infty(M), \ X_h := \ed h^\sharp\ {}$ is the *Hamiltonian vector field generated by* $h$. 
$~$
$\omega$ determines a Poisson bracket 
$$
\{ f, h \} := \omega(X_f, X_h) = X_h(f). %{\cal L}_{X_h} f.
%[ \alpha, \beta ]_{\dcXM} := \omega(\alpha^\sharp, \beta^\sharp) = \alpha(\beta^\sharp).
$$

---

***Cotangent bundles:*** The *canonical 1-form* $\, \theta_0\, {}$ on $\, T^*Q\, {}$ is given by
$$
\theta_0(w_{z_p}) := z_p(d_{z_p} \pi(w_{z_p})) \qquad \quad \forall \ w_{z_p} \in T_{z_p} T^* Q, \ z_p \in T^* Q,
$$

where $\, \pi: T^* Q \to Q\, {}$ denotes the projection to the base
$$
\pi(z_q) = q \qquad \quad \forall \ z_g \in T^*_q Q, \ q \in Q,
$$

The *canonical symplectic structure* on $T^*Q$ is 
$$
\omega_0 := - \ed \theta_0.
$$
$~$

### Fill in some of the gaps in verifying the Jacobi identity from Poisson brackets

Preliminary to proving that $\ \{ \ , \ \}_{C^\infty(M)} \ {}$ satisfies the Jacobi identity, show that:
$$
d \{ f, h \}_{C^\infty(M)} = [df, dh]_{\dcXM}
$$
$~$

---

Invoke the following identity from the "cheat sheet" (see Resources module), applied to $\omega$ and $X, Y, Z \in \cXM$:
$$\eqa{
\ed \omega(X, Y, Z) &= X(\omega(Y, Z)) - \omega(Y, [X, Z]) \\
&\qquad + \text{cyclic permutations of } X, Y, Z.
}
$$


Let $X = \alpha^\sharp$ and $Y = \beta^\sharp$.

$$\eqa{
\alpha^\sharp(\omega(\beta^\sharp, Z)) 
&= \alpha^\sharp(\beta(Z)) \\
&= ({\cal L}_{\alpha^\sharp}\beta)(Z) + \beta([\alpha^\sharp, Z]) \\
&= ({\cal L}_{\alpha^\sharp}\beta)(Z) + \omega(\beta^\sharp, [\alpha^\sharp, Z]) 
}
$$
$\ \Longrightarrow$
$$
\alpha^\sharp(\omega(\beta^\sharp, Z)) - \omega(\beta^\sharp, [\alpha^\sharp, Z]) = ({\cal L}_{\alpha^\sharp}\beta)(Z).
$$

Analogously,

---

$$\eqa{\beta^\sharp(\omega(Z, \alpha^\sharp)) - \omega(\alpha^\sharp, [Z, \beta^\sharp])&= - (\beta^\sharp(\omega(\alpha^\sharp, Z)) - \omega(\alpha^\sharp, [\beta^\sharp, Z]))  \\
&= - ({\cal L}_{\beta^\sharp}\alpha)(Z).
}
$$

Finally,
$$\eqa{
Z(\omega(X, Y)) - \omega(Z, [Y, X]) &= Z(\omega(\alpha^\sharp, \beta^\sharp)) + \omega([\alpha^\sharp, \beta^\sharp], Z)\\
&= \lp d(\omega(\alpha^\sharp, \beta^\sharp))+ [ \alpha , \beta ]_{\dcXM} \rp(Z).
}
$$

$\omega\, {}$ closed $\ \Longrightarrow \ \ed \omega(\alpha^\sharp, \beta^\sharp, Z) = 0. \quad$ 
$~$

Putting it all together, dropping $Z$, and rearranging terms gives
$$
[ \alpha , \beta ]_{\dcXM} = {\cal L}_{\beta^\sharp}\alpha - {\cal L}_{\alpha^\sharp}\beta - d(\omega(\alpha^\sharp, \beta^\sharp)).
$$

---

Now show that the space $\, {\cal X^*_C}(M)\, {}$ of closed 1-forms is a Lie subalgebra.

The identity ("Cartan's magic formula")
$$
{\cal L}_X \alpha = \iota_X \ed \alpha + \ed \iota_X \alpha
$$
$\ \Longrightarrow\ {}$ Lie derivatives of closed forms are exact. 
$~$
If $\alpha$ is a closed 1-form,
$$
\omega(\alpha^\sharp, \beta^\sharp) = \alpha(\beta^\sharp) 
\qquad \Longrightarrow\qquad  {\cal L}_{\beta^\sharp} \alpha = \ed \iota_{\beta^\sharp} \alpha 
= d \iota_{\beta^\sharp}\iota_{\alpha^\sharp}\omega.
$$
$~$
$\alpha\, {}$ and $\, \beta \, {}$ closed $\ \Longrightarrow\ {}$
$$\eqa{
[ \alpha , \beta ]_{\dcXM} &= {\cal L}_{\beta^\sharp}\alpha - {\cal L}_{\alpha^\sharp}\beta - d\iota_{\beta^\sharp}\iota_{\alpha^\sharp}\omega \\
&= - d \iota_{\beta^\sharp}\iota_{\alpha^\sharp}\omega.
}
$$

---

Setting $\, \alpha = df \, {}$ and $\, \beta = dh\,{}$ gives
$$\eqa{
[ df, dh ]_{\dcXM} &= d \iota_{dh^\sharp}\iota_{df^\sharp}\omega \\
&= d \iota_{X_h}\iota_{X_f}\omega \\
 &= d \{f, h \}.
}
$$
$~$
Applying ${}^\sharp\,{}$ to both sides of the previous equality gives
$$\eqa{
[X_f, X_h] &= [ df, dh ]_{\dcXM}^\sharp\\ 
&= \lp d \{f, h \} \rp^\sharp \\
&= X_{\{f, h \}}.
}
$$ 
We showed last week that satisfaction of this equality for all $f, h$ is equivalent to satisfaction of the Jacobi identity by $\{\ , \ \}$.

***More recap: The symplectic structures on $T^*G$ and $G \times \fg^*$*** 

Given $\, \xi \in \fg, \ \mu \in \fg^*, \ \text{and}\ \lozenge = L\ {}$ or $\ R, {}$ the $\lozenge$-invariant vector fields and 1-forms
$$
\xi_\lozenge(g) := d_1 \lozenge(\xi) \sands 
\mu_\lozenge(g) := (d_g \lozenge_{g^{-1}})^* \mu.
%, \sands \Omega^\lozenge_\mu := \Omega_{\mu_\lozenge}.
$$
determine a diffeomorphism
$$\eqa{
\tau_\lozenge: G \times& \fg^* \times \fg \times \fg^* \to T(T^*G) \\ 
(g, \mu, \xi, \nu) &\mapsto d_g \mu_\lozenge(\xi_\lozenge(g)) + \text{vert}_{\mu_\lozenge(g)}(\nu_\lozenge(g))\in T_{\mu_\lozenge(g)}T^*G.
}
$$
$~$
The canonical symplectic structure on $T^*G$ satisfies
$$
%\omega_\lozenge(g, \mu)((\xi_\lozenge(g), \nu), ((\tilde \xi_\lozenge(g), \tilde \nu)
\omega_0(\mu_\lozenge(g))(\tau_\lozenge(g, \mu, \xi, \nu), \tau_\lozenge(g, \mu, \tilde \xi,\tilde \nu))
 = \tilde \nu(\xi) - \nu(\tilde \xi) \pm_\lozenge \mu([\xi,  \tilde \xi])
$$
and determines symplectic structures $\, \omega_\lozenge\ {}$ on $G \times \fg^*$.

### $G$-invariant functions on $T^*G$ and the Lie-Poisson bracket 

$f\in C^\infty(\fg^*)\ {}$ determines a function $\tilde f$ on $\ G \times \fg^*\ {}$ with trivial $G$ dependence
$$
\tilde f(g, \mu) := f(\mu)
$$
 satisfying
$$\eqa{
 \fd {\tilde f} g(g, \mu)(\xi) + \nu(\fd {\tilde f} \mu(g, \mu)) &=  d \tilde f(g, \mu)(\xi_\lozenge(g), \nu) \\
&= df(\mu)(x\nu) \\
&= \nu(\fd f \mu(\mu)).
}
$$
The Poisson bracket of two $G$-invariant functions on $G \times \fg^*$ equals their Lie-Poisson bracket, regarded as functions on $\fg^*$, with $\pm$ determined by the handedness of $\lozenge$
$$
\{\tilde f, \tilde h \}_\lozenge(g, \mu) = \{f, h\}_\pm(\mu).
$$

---

The IVP 
$$
(\dot g, \dot \mu) = X_{\tilde h}(g, \mu) = \lp \lp \fd f \mu(\mu) \rp_{\!\lozenge}(g), \pm \ad^*_{\fd f \mu(\mu)} \mu \rp,
$$
with initial data $g(0) = g_0, \ \mu(0) = \mu_0, \ {}$ is partially decoupled. 

$~$
The solution $\mu: I \to \fg^*$ of the Euler equation
$$
\dot  \mu = \pm \ad^*_{\fd f \mu(\mu)} \mu \sands \mu(0) = \mu_0,
$$
determines a time-dependent IVP
$$
\dot g = d_1 \lozenge_g(\fd f \mu(\mu(t))) \sands g(0) = g_0.
$$

Finding $\, g(t)\, {}$ given $\, \mu(t)\, {}$ is one version of a process called *reconstruction*.

### Momentum maps 

Assume that a Lie group $G$ acts on the symplectic manifold $M$ and preserves the symplectic structure, i.e.
$$
\rho(g)^* \omega = \omega \qquad \qquad \forall \ g \in G.
$$
$~$
Given a map $J: M \to \fg^* \ {}$ and $\xi \in \fg, \ {}$ define $J_\xi \in C^\infty(M)\, {}$ by
$$
J_\xi(p) := J(p)(\xi).
$$
$~$
$J$ is a *momentum map* for the $G$ action on $M$ if
$$
X_{J_\xi} = \xi_M \qquad \qquad \forall \ \xi \in \fg,
$$
where $\xi_M(p)  = \dep {\exp(\epsilon \, \xi) \cdot p}$ is the infinitesimal generator determined by $\xi$.

---

***Claim:*** If $h \in C^\infty(M)\, {}$ is $G$-invariant, then $J$ is preserved by the flow of $X_h$.

*Verify:* 
$$\eqa{
\calL_{X_h} J_\xi &= \omega(X_{J_\xi}, X_h) \\
&= - \omega(X_h, X_{J_\xi}) \\
&= - \calL_{\xi_M}h \\
&= \dep{h \circ \rho(\epsilon \, \xi)}\\
&= \dep{h} \\
& = 0. \phantom{\int_\int}}
$$

*Very important example:* $M = T^*Q, \ {}$ where $G$ acts on $Q$, and the action of $G$ on $T^*Q$ is the cotangent lifted action
$$
g \cdot z_q = (d_{g \cdot q}\rho(g^{-1}))^* z_q.
$$

---

***Claim:*** $\theta_0$ and $\omega_0$ are $G$-invariant, and 
$$
J_\xi(z_q) := z_q(\xi_Q(q)) 
$$
determines an equivariant momentum map $J$ on $T^*Q$, i.e.
$$
J(g \cdot z_q) = \Ad^*_{g^{-1}} J(z_q).
$$
$~$
*Verify:* $\ \pi \circ P(g) = \rho(g) \circ \pi \ \Longrightarrow$

$$\eqa{
(P(g)^*\theta_0)(z_q)(w_{z_q}) 
&= (g  \cdot z_q)(d_{g  \cdot z_q} \pi( d_{z_q} P(g)(w_{z_q}))\\
&= (g \cdot z_q)( d_{z_q} (\pi \circ P(g))(w_{z_q})\\
&= (d_{g \cdot q}\rho(g^{-1}))^* z_q(d_{z_q}(\rho(g) \circ \pi)(w_{z_q}))\\
&= z_q(d_{z_q} \pi(w_{z_q}))\\
&= \theta_0(z_q)(w_{z_q}).
}
$$

---

Hence $\theta_0$ is $G$-invariant.

$$\eqa{
P(g)^*\omega_0 &= - P(g)^*(\ed \theta_0) \\
&= - \ed (P(g)^*\theta_0) \\
&= - \ed \theta_0 \\
&= \omega_0
}
$$
$\Longrightarrow \ \omega_0$ is $G$-invariant, too.
$~$
$$
d_{z_q} \pi( \xi_{T^*Q}(z_q)) = \xi_Q(q) \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad 
J_\xi = \iota_{\xi_{T^*Q}} \theta_0,
$$
and hence 
$$\eqa{
d J_\xi &= d \iota_{\xi_{T^*Q}} \theta_0 \\
&= {\cal L}_{\xi_{T^*Q}} \theta_0 - \iota_{\xi_{T^*Q}} \ed \theta_0\\
&= \iota_{\xi_{T^*Q}} \omega_0.
}
$$


---

***Claim:*** The momentum map $J: T^*Q \to \fg^*\ {}$ described above is $G$-equivariant, i.e.
$$
J(g \cdot z_q) = \Ad_{g^{-1}}^* J(z_q) \qquad \qquad \forall \ g \in G, \ z_q \in T^*Q.
$$

*Verify:*
$$\eqa{
J(g \cdot z_q)(\xi) &= J_\xi(g \cdot z_q) \\
&= (g \cdot z_q)(\xi_Q(\pi((g \cdot z_q))))\\
&= z_q (d_{g \cdot q}\rho(g^{-1})(\xi_Q(g \cdot q)))\\
&= z_q (\underbrace{\rho(g)^*\xi_Q}_{(\Ad_{g^{-1}} \xi)_Q}(q))\\
&= J_{\Ad_{g^{-1}} \xi}(z_q) \\
&= \Ad_{g^{-1}}^* J(z_q)(\xi)
}
$$
$\Longrightarrow \ J$ is $G$-equivariant.

---

*Consequence:* $J$ maps $G$-orbits in $T^*Q$ onto coadjoint orbits in $\fg^*$.
$~$

***Claim:***(without proof here) If $G$ acts on a symplectic manifold $M$, 
- there is an equivariant momentum map $J : M \to \fg^*$ associated to the $G$ action, 
$\mu$ is a regular value of $J$, and
- the restriction of the $G$-action to $\, J^{-1}(\mu)\, {}$ is free and proper, then 

$J^{-1}(\mu)/G_\mu \, {}$ is a symplectic manifold, with symplectic structure $\omega_\mu$ satisfying
$$
\pi_\mu^* \omega_\mu = \iota_\mu^* \omega,
$$
where 
$$
\pi_\mu: J^{-1}(\mu) \to J^{-1}(\mu)/G_\mu \sands \iota_\mu: J^{-1}(\mu) \to M
\sands 
$$
denote the quotient and inclusion maps.
$~$
This generalizes a classical, coordinate based construction for actions of abelian groups.

*Reconstruction:* As for the "full version" of the Euler equations, we can find solutions of the smaller dynamical system, and then solve for the remaining "groupy" information.

There are Poisson versions of momentum maps as well:

If $M$ is a Poisson manifold and $G$ acts on $M$ by Poisson maps, then (as for the symplectic case) $\ J: M \to \fg^* \ {}$ is a *momentum map* for the $G$ action on $M$ if
$$
X_{J_\xi} = \xi_M \qquad \qquad \forall \ \xi \in \fg.
$$
$~$

### Relative equilibria

If $G$ acts on a symplectic (resp. Poisson) manifold by symplectic (resp. Poisson) maps and $h \in C^\infty(M), \ {}$ is $G$ invariant, then $p \in M$ is a *relative equilibrium with generator $\xi$* of the Hamiltonian system determined by $h$ if 
$$
X_h(p) = \xi_M(p).
$$

---

If $p$ is a relative equilibrium with generator $\xi$ and $\calF_t$ denotes the flow at time $t$ of $X_h$, then 
$$
\calF_t(p) = \exp(t \, \xi) \cdot p.
$$
$~$
If $J: M \to \fg^*$ is a momentum map for the action of $G$ on a symplectic manifold $M$, then $p$ is a relative equilibrium with generator $\xi \ \ \Longleftrightarrow \ \ p\ {}$ is a critical point of $h - J_\xi$, since
$$
X_h - \xi_M = (d (h - J_\xi))^\sharp,
$$
and $\ {}^\sharp\ {}$ is injective.
$~$
If $J: M \to \fg^*$ is a momentum map for the action of $G$ on a Poisson manifold $M$, then $p$ is a relative equilibrium with generator $\xi \ \ \Longleftrightarrow \ \ p\ {}$ is a critical point of 
$$h - J_\xi - C$$ 
for some Casimir $C$.
