\newcommand{\eqa}[1]{\begin{align} #1 \end{align}}$Much of the "early" development of mathematical modeling was based on the belief/assertion that Nature optimizes. 

If scientists could figure out what Nature was optimizing, they could explain why things work the way they do, and make accurate predictions. 

That approach led to the calculus of variations, among other things.

Calculus of variations involves finding the critical points of functions on spaces of paths. 

If the underlying space is finite dimensional, this leads to ODEs determining critical paths.
If the space is infinite dimensional, we get PDEs.

"Modern" geometric constructions, including symplectic and Poisson geometry, yield these DEs or close relatives, and provide arguably easier access to some key information.

### Symplectic manifolds and Hamiltonian dynamics

A closed, non-degenerate 2-form on a smooth manifold $M$ is called a *symplectic structure*, and the manifold $M$ is said to be *symplectic*.
$~$
Given $\, (M, \omega), \ {}$ define ${\ }^\flat: \cXM \to \dcXM\,{}$ and ${\ }^\sharp: \dcXM \to \cXM\,{}$ by
$$
X^\flat := \iota_X \omega \sands \iota_{\beta^\sharp} \omega = \beta.
$$
$~$
Given $\, h \in C^\infty(M), \ X_h := \ed h^\sharp\ {}$ is the *Hamiltonian vector field generated by* $h$. 
$~$
***Claim:*** $\omega$ determines a Poisson bracket 
$$
\{ f, h \} := \omega(X_f, X_h) = {\cal L}_{X_h} f.
%[ \alpha, \beta ]_{\dcXM} := \omega(\alpha^\sharp, \beta^\sharp) = \alpha(\beta^\sharp).
$$
$~$

---

$\{·, h\}\, {}$ is a derivation, and the Hamiltonian vector fields determined by $\,\omega\,$ and $\, \{·, ·\}\, {}$ 
are equal, since
$$
X_h(f) = \iota_{X_h} df = \iota_{X_h}\iota_{X_f}\omega = \{f,h\}  
$$
for all $\, f, h \in C^\infty(M)$.

To show that $\, \{·, ·\}\, {}$ satisfies the Jacobi identity, we'll introduce another bracket.
$~$
$\omega$ also determines a Lie bracket on the space $\, \dcXM \, {}$ of smooth 1-forms:
$$
[ \alpha, \beta ]_{\dcXM} := - [\alpha^\sharp, \beta^\sharp]^\flat.
$$

- The space $\, {\cal X^*_C}(M)\, {}$ of closed 1-forms is a Lie subalgebra, and 
- The derived algebra of $\,  {\cal X^*_C}(M)\, {}$ is contained in the subalgebra $\,  {\cal X^*_E}(M)\, {}$ 
of exact 1-forms.

---

Setting $\, \alpha = df \, {}$ and $\, \beta = dh\,{}$ gives
$$\eqa{
[ df, dh ]_{\dcXM} &= d \iota_{dh^\sharp}\iota_{df^\sharp}\omega \\
&= d \iota_{X_h}\iota_{X_f}\omega \\
 &= d \{f, h \}.
}
$$

Applying ${}^\sharp\,{}$ to both sides of the previous equality gives
$$\eqa{
[X_f, X_h] &= [ df, dh ]_{\dcXM}^\sharp\\ 
&= \lp d \{f, h \} \rp^\sharp \\
&= X_{\{f, h \}}.
}
$$ 

$\, \{·, ·\}\, {}$ satisfies the Jacobi identity $\quad  \Longleftrightarrow$
$$
[X_f , X_h] = X_{\{f,h\}} \qquad \qquad \forall\ f, h \in C^\infty(M),
$$
so $\, \{·, ·\}\, {}$ is a Poisson bracket.

### The standard symplectic structure on $\R^{2n}$

$\R^{2n}$, with coordinates $(q_1, \ldots, q_n, p_1, \ldots, p_n)$, has a "standard" symplectic structure
$$
ω = ∑_{𝑖=1}^n dp_i ∧ dq_i
$$
$~$
Hamilton's equations with respect to this structure are
$$
\dot q_i = \frac {∂h}{∂𝑝_𝑖} \sands 
\dot p_i = - \frac {∂h}{∂q_𝑖}, \qquad i = 1, \ldots, n.
$$
$~$
The associated Poisson bracket is 
$$
\{f, g\}=∑_{𝑖=1}^n \frac {∂f}{∂𝑞_𝑖} \frac {∂f}{∂𝑝_𝑖} − \frac {∂g}{∂𝑞_𝑖} \frac {∂f}{∂𝑝_𝑖}.
$$

### The canonical symplectic structure on a cotangent bundle

If $\, \pi: T^* Q \to Q\, {}$ denotes the projection to the base
$$
\pi(z_q) = q \qquad \quad \forall \ z_g \in T^*_q Q, \ q \in Q,
$$
the *canonical 1-form* $\, \theta_0\, {}$ on $\, T^*Q\, {}$ is given by
$$
\theta_0(w_{z_p}) := z_p(d_{z_p} \pi(w_{z_p})) \qquad \quad \forall \ w_{z_p} \in T_{z_p} T^* Q, \ z_p \in T^* Q.
$$
$~$
The *canonical symplectic structure* on $T^*Q$ is 
$$
\omega_0 := - \ed \theta_0.
$$
$~$
***Claim:*** If $\,\beta\, {}$ is a 1-form on $\,Q,\ {}$ then 
$$
\beta^*\theta_0 = \beta \qquad \text{and hence}\qquad \beta^*\omega_0 = - \ed \beta.
$$
$~$

---

*Verify:* Given $\ v_p \in T^*Q,$
$$

$$
$$\eqa{
\pi \circ \beta = \text{id}_{Q}\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
\beta^*\theta_0(p)(v_p) &= \beta(p)(d_{z_p} \pi(d_p \beta (v_p)))\qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \\
&= \beta(p)(d_p (\pi\circ\beta) (v_p)) \\
&= \beta(p)(v_p).
}
$$

Since pullback commutes with exterior differentiation,
$$\eqa{
\beta^*\omega_0 &= \beta^*(- \ed \theta_0) \\
&= - \ed (\beta^*\theta_0) \\
&= - \ed \beta.
}
$$
$~$
We can put this to work in calculations, constructing an isomorphism
$$
 T_q Q \times T_q^* Q \approx T_{z_q}T^*Q.
$$

---

$$\eqa{
\text{vert}_{z_q}: T_q^*Q &\to \ker \, d_{z_q} \pi \\
p_q & \mapsto\smallfrac {d\ }{d \epsilon} (z_q + \epsilon  \, p_q)|_{\epsilon = 0}
}
$$
is an isomorphism, called the *vertical lift* at $z_q$.
$~$
Given $\ w_{z_q} \in T_{z_q}T^*Q, \ {}$ choose a 1-form $\beta$ such that $\ \beta(q) = z_q$. 
$$
v_q := d_{z_q} \pi(w_{z_q}) \in T_q Q\phantom{X^{\int^\int}}
$$
is the *horizontal component* of $\ w_{z_q} \in T_{z_q}T^*Q.\ {}$
$~$
$$
\text{vert}_{z_q}^{-1}(w_{z_q} -  d_q \beta(v_q) ) \in T_q ^*Q \phantom{X_\int^\int}
$$
is the *vertical component of $\ w_{z_q}\ {}$ with respect to* $\beta$.

---

$$\eqa{
    \hat \beta_q : T_q Q \times T_q^* Q &\to T_{\beta(q)} T^* Q \\
(v_q, p_q) &\mapsto d_q \beta(v_q) + \text{vert}_{\beta(q)}(p_q)
}
$$
is an isomorphism satisfying

$$
\omega_0(\beta(q))(\hat \beta_q(v_q, p_q), \hat \beta_q(\tilde v_q, \tilde p_q)) = 
\tilde p_q(v_q) - p_q(\tilde v_q) -  \ed \beta(q)(v_q, \tilde v_q).
$$

### The symplectic structure on $G \times \fg^*$ induced by global trivialization of $T^*G$ 

The natural choices for 1-forms $\beta$ on a Lie group $G$ are the left or right invariant 1-forms determined by elements of $\fg^*$.

$~$

Given $\, \xi \in \fg, \ \mu \in \fg^*, \ \text{and}\ \lozenge = L\ {}$ or $\ R, {}$ define
$$
\xi_\lozenge(g) := d_1 \lozenge(\xi) \sands 
\mu_\lozenge(g) := (d_g \lozenge_{g^{-1}})^* \mu.
%, \sands \Omega^\lozenge_\mu := \Omega_{\mu_\lozenge}.
$$

$~$

$TG$ and $T^*G$ are trivial bundles, with diffeomorphisms 
$$\eqa{
\phi_\lozenge: G \times \fg &\to TG \qquad \qquad  \psi_\lozenge: G \times \fg^* \to T^*G \\ 
(g, \xi) &\mapsto  \xi_\lozenge(g)\ \ \qquad \qquad \quad (g, \mu) \mapsto \mu_\lozenge(g).
}
$$
$~$
$~$
$~$

---

Using these maps, we can construct a diffeomorphism
$$\eqa{
\tau_\lozenge: G \times& \fg^* \times \fg \times \fg^* \to T(T^*G) \\ 
(g, \mu, \xi, \nu) &\mapsto \widehat {\mu_\lozenge}_g(\xi_\lozenge(g), \nu_\lozenge(g) ) \\
&= d_g \mu_\lozenge(\xi_\lozenge(g)) + \text{vert}_{\mu_\lozenge(g)}(\nu_\lozenge(g))\in T_{\mu_\lozenge(g)}T^*G.
}
$$
$~$
***Claim:*** The canonical symplectic structure on $T^*G$ satisfies
$$
%\omega_\lozenge(g, \mu)((\xi_\lozenge(g), \nu), ((\tilde \xi_\lozenge(g), \tilde \nu)
\omega_0(\mu_\lozenge(g))(\tau_\lozenge(g, \mu, \xi, \nu), \tau_\lozenge(g, \mu, \tilde \xi,\tilde \nu))
%= \Omega_{\mu_\lozenge}(g)((\xi_\lozenge(g), \nu_\lozenge(g)), (\tilde \xi_\lozenge(g), \tilde \nu_\lozenge(g)))
 = \tilde \nu(\xi) - \nu(\tilde \xi) \pm \mu([\xi,  \tilde \xi])
$$
for all $g \in G, \ \xi, \tilde \xi \in \fg, \ \mu, \nu, \tilde \nu \in \fg^*$.
$~$
The sign of the last term is determined by the handedness of $\lozenge$.
$~$
$~$

---

*Verify:*
$$\eqa{
\iota_{\xi_\lozenge}\tilde \nu_\lozenge(g)
&= ((d_g \lozenge_{g^{-1}})^* \tilde \nu)(d_1 \lozenge_g(\xi))\\
&= \tilde \nu(d_g \lozenge_{g^{-1}}(d_1 \lozenge_g(\xi))\\
&= \tilde \nu(\xi).
}
$$

The identity (see, e.g., the Lie derivatives and exterior derivatives cheat sheet)
$$
\iota_Y \iota_X \ed \beta = {\cal L}_Y (\iota_X \beta) - {\cal L}_X (\iota_Y \beta)
- \iota_{[X, Y]} \beta
$$
for arbitrary vector fields $X$ and $Y$, and 1-form $\beta$ implies 
$$
\iota_{ \tilde \xi_\lozenge} \iota_{\xi_\lozenge} \ed \mu_\lozenge = - \iota_{[\xi_\lozenge, \tilde \xi_\lozenge]} \mu_\lozenge = \pm \mu([\xi, \tilde \xi]).
$$

$~$
The symplectic structure $\omega_\lozenge$ on $G \times \fg^*$ determined by $\omega_0$ on $T^*G$ is
$$
\omega_\lozenge(g, \mu)((\xi_\lozenge(g), \nu), (\tilde \xi_\lozenge(g), \tilde \nu)
%\omega_0(\mu_\lozenge(g))(\tau_\lozenge(g, \mu, \xi, \nu), \tau_\lozenge(g, \mu, \tilde \xi,\tilde \nu))
%= \Omega_{\mu_\lozenge}(g)((\xi_\lozenge(g), \nu_\lozenge(g)), (\tilde \xi_\lozenge(g), \tilde \nu_\lozenge(g)))
 = \tilde \nu(\xi) - \nu(\tilde \xi) \pm \mu([\xi,  \tilde \xi]).
$$

### Hamiltonian vector fields on $G \times \fg^*$

Given $f \in C^\infty(G \times \fg^*), \ {}$ and a choice of $\lozenge, \ \exists!{}$ 
$$
\fd f g: G \times \fg^* \to \fg^* \sands \fd f \mu: G \times \fg^* \to \fg
$$
satisfying
$$
df(g, \mu)(\xi_\lozenge(g), \nu) = \fd f g(g, \mu)(\xi) + \nu(\fd f \mu(g, \mu))
$$
$\forall \ g \in G, \ \xi \in \fg, \ \mu, \nu\in \fg^*$.
$~$
***Claim:*** The Hamiltonian vector field $X_h$ of $h \in C^\infty(G \times \fg^*)\ {}$ is
$$
X_h(g, \mu) = \lp d_1 \lozenge_g\lp \fd f \mu(g, \mu) \rp, - \fd f g(g, \mu) \pm \ad^*_{\fd f \mu(g, \mu)} \mu \rp.
$$

---

*Verify:* If $\ \xi_h: G \times \fg^* \to \fg\ {}$ and $\ \nu_h: G \times \fg^* \to \fg^*\ {}$ satisfy
$$
X_h(g, \mu) = (d_1 \lozenge_g(\xi_h(g, \mu)), \nu_h(g, \mu)),\phantom{X^\int}
$$
so that
$$\eqa{
\fd h g(g, \mu)(\eta) + \nu(\fd h \mu(g, \mu)) &= 
dh(g, \mu)(\eta_\lozenge(g), \nu) \\
&= \omega_\lozenge(g, \mu)(X_h(g, \mu),  (\eta_\lozenge(g), \nu))\\
&= \nu(\xi_h) - \nu_h(\eta) \pm \underbrace{\mu([\xi_h, \eta])}_{\ad^*_{\xi_h}\mu( \eta)}
}
$$
$\forall \ g \in G, \ \xi, \eta \in \fg, \ \mu, \nu \in \fg^*, \ {}$ it follows that
$~$
$$
\xi_h(g, \mu) = \fd h \mu(g, \mu) \sands \nu_h(g, \mu) = - \fd h g(g, \mu) \pm \ad^*_{\xi_h(g, \mu)}\mu.
$$

### The Poisson bracket on $C^\infty(G \times \fg^*)$

$$\eqa{
\{f, h\}_\lozenge(g, \mu) &= \omega_\lozenge(X_f, X_h)(g, \mu) \phantom{int^{\int^\int}} \\
&= \nu_h(\xi_f) - \nu_f(\xi_h) \pm \mu([\xi_f, \xi_h]) \\
&=  \fd f g(\fd h \mu) - \fd h g(\fd f \mu)\pm \mu \lp \left [\fd f \mu, \fd h \mu \right ] \rp,
}
$$
where all derivatives are evaluated at $(g, \mu)$.
$~$
***Example.*** The standard Poisson bracket on $\R^{2n}$
$$
\textstyle \{f, g\}=∑_{𝑖=1}^n \frac {∂f}{∂𝑞_𝑖} \frac {∂f}{∂𝑝_𝑖} − \frac {∂g}{∂𝑞_𝑖} \frac {∂f}{∂𝑝_𝑖}.
$$
can be regarded as a special case, with abelian $G = \fg = \R^n, \ {}$ 
$$
\textstyle \fd f g = \lp \frac {∂f}{∂𝑞_1}, \ldots, \frac {∂f}{∂𝑞_n} \rp,
\sands
\fd f \mu = \lp \frac {∂f}{∂p_1}, \ldots, \frac {∂f}{∂p_n} \rp.
$$
$~$

### $G$-invariant functions on $T^*G$ and the Lie-Poisson bracket 

$f \in C^\infty(\fg^*)\ {}$ determines a function $\tilde f$ on $\ G \times \fg^*\ {}$ with trivial $G$ dependence, 
$$
\tilde f(g, \mu) := f(\mu) ,
$$
with partial derivative satisfying
$$\eqa{
 \fd {\tilde f} g(g, \mu)(\xi) + \nu(\fd {\tilde f} \mu(g, \mu)) &=  d \tilde f(g, \mu)(\xi_\lozenge(g), \nu) \\
&= df(\mu)(x\nu) \\
&= \nu(\fd f \mu(\mu)),
}
$$
for all $g \in G$, $\xi \in \fg$, and $\mu, \nu \in \fg^*$. 

It follows that
$$
\{\tilde f, \tilde h \}_\lozenge(g, \mu) = \{f, h\}_\pm(\mu).
$$

---
The IVP 
$$
(\dot g, \dot \mu) = X_{\tilde h}(g, \mu) = \lp \lp \fd h \mu(\mu) \rp_{\!\lozenge}(g), \pm \ad^*_{\fd fh\mu(\mu)} \mu \rp,
$$
with initial data $g(0) = g_0, \ \mu(0) = \mu_0, \ {}$ is partially decoupled. 
$~$

A solution $\mu: (a, b) \to \fg^*$ of the Euler equation
$$
\dot  \mu = \pm \ad^*_{\fd h \mu(\mu)} \mu \sands \mu(0) = \mu_0,
$$
determines a (typically non-autonomous) first order IVP
$$
\dot g = d_1 \blacklozenge_g(\fd h \mu(\mu(t))) \sands g(0) = g_0
$$
on $G$.
$~$

---

Finding $\, g(t)\, {}$ given $\, \mu(t)\, {}$ is one version of a process called *reconstruction*.

If $G \subseteq GL(n, F), \ {}$ the ODE for $g$ can be regarded as a linear ODE on $\, F^{n \times n}$.
$~$
If $\mu_0$ is an equilibrium of the Euler equation, i.e. $\ \ad^*_{\fd h \mu(\mu)} \mu = 0, \ {}$ then
$$
g(t) = \lozenge_{g_0}(\exp(t \, \xi)), \qquad \text{where} \quad \xi = \fd h \mu(\mu_0),
$$
and $g_0$ is called a *relative equilibrium*.
$~$
More generally, if $G$ acts on a Poisson (resp. symplectic) manifold by Poisson (resp. symplectic) maps, then $p_0 \in M$ is a *relative equilibrium* of the Hamiltonian system determined by a $G$-invariant function $h$ if 
$$
p(t) := \exp(t \, \xi)\cdot p_0 \qquad \text{satisfies}\qquad \dot p = X_h(p).
$$

---

***Example:*** $G = SO(3, \R)\ {}$ and $\ h(\mu)  = \smallfrac 1 2 \mu(I^{-1} \mu)$
$~$
$$
\dot \mu = \mp \ad^*_{I^{-1} \mu} \mu = \pm \mu \times (I^{-1} \mu)
$$
$\Longrightarrow \quad \mu_0 \ {}$ is an equilibrium of Euler's equation $\ \Longleftrightarrow \ \mu_0\ {}$ is a eigenvector of $I^{-1}$. 
$~$
$g(t)\, {}$ is a constant speed rotation about the axis $\mu_0$. 
$~$

### Geodesics as Hamiltonian systems

Minimization of length
$$
\int_a^b |\gamma'(t)|_{{\rm g}(\gamma(t))}\ dt
$$
of a parametrized curve $\gamma: (a, b) \to M$ is equivalent to minimization of kinetic energy
$$
E(\gamma) := \frac{1}{2} \int_a^b |\gamma'(t)|_{{\rm g}(\gamma(t))}^2\ dt
$$
over constant speed parametrized curves.

The variational criticality condition on parametrized paths are equivalent (if appropriate technical conditions are satisfied) to the condition that critical paths be solutions of the *Euler-Lagrange equation*.

---

The *Euler-Lagrange equation* is a second order DE on $M$ that is equivalent to the first order DE on $TM$ determined by Hamilton's equations with respect to the pullback of the canonical symplectic structure on $T^*M$ to $TM$ by $\flat$.   
$~$
If $M = G$ and the Riemannian metric is left (or right) invariant, then projections onto the first factor of solutions of 
$$
(\dot g, \dot \mu) = X_{\tilde h}(g, \mu) = \lp \lp \fd h \mu(\mu) \rp_{\!\lozenge}(g), \pm \ad^*_{\fd h \mu(\mu)} \mu \rp,
$$
with
$$\tilde h(g, \mu) = h(\mu) = \half |\mu|^2_{\fg^*}, \phantom{x_\int}
$$
satisfy the Euler-Lagrange equations, and hence also satisfy the geodesic equation.
