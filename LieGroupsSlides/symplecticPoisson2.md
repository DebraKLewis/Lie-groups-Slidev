### Hamiltonian vector fields on $G \times \fg^*$

Given $f \in C^\infty(G \times \fg^*), \ {}$ and a choice of $\lozenge, \ \exists!{}$ 
$$
\fd f g: G \times \fg^* \to \fg^* \sands \fd f \mu: G \times \fg^* \to \fg
$$
satisfying
$$
df(g, \mu)(\xi_\lozenge(g), \nu) = \fd f g(g, \mu)(\xi) + \nu(\fd f \mu(g, \mu))
$$
$\forall \ g \in G, \ \xi \in \fg, \ \mu, \nu\in \fg^*$.
$~$
***Claim:*** The Hamiltonian vector field $X_h$ of $h \in C^\infty(G \times \fg^*)\ {}$ is
$$
X_h(g, \mu) = \lp d_1 \lozenge_g\lp \fd f \mu(g, \mu) \rp, - \fd f g(g, \mu) \pm \ad^*_{\fd f \mu(g, \mu)} \mu \rp.
$$

*Verify:* Let $\ \xi_h: G \times \fg^* \to \fg\ {}$ and $\ \nu_h: G \times \fg^* \to \fg^*\ {}$ satisfy
$$
X_h(g, \mu) = (d_1 \lozenge_g(\xi_h(g, \mu)), \nu_h(g, \mu)).
$$

---

$$\eqa{
\fd h g(g, \mu)(\eta) + \nu(\fd h \mu(g, \mu)) &= 
dh(g, \mu)(\eta_\lozenge(g), \nu) \\
&= \omega_\lozenge(g, \mu)(X_h(g, \mu),  (\eta_\lozenge(g), \nu))\\
&= \nu(\xi_h) - \nu_h(\eta) \pm \underbrace{\mu([\xi_h, \eta])}_{\ad^*_{\xi_h}\mu( \eta)}
}
$$
$\forall \ g \in G, \ \xi, \eta \in \fg, \ \mu, \nu \in \fg^* \quad \Longrightarrow$
$$
\xi_h(g, \mu) = \fd h \mu(g, \mu) \sands \nu_h(g, \mu) = - \fd h g(g, \mu) \pm \ad^*_{\xi_h(g, \mu)}\mu.
$$
$~$
***Poisson bracket on $C^\infty(G \times \fg^*)$***

$$\eqa{
\{f, h\}_\lozenge(g, \mu) &= \omega_\lozenge(X_f, X_h) \\
&= \nu_h(\xi_f) - \nu_f(\xi_h) \pm \mu([\xi_f, \xi_h]) \\
&=  \fd f g(\fd h \mu) - \fd h g(\fd f \mu)\pm \mu \lp \left [\fd f \mu, \fd h \mu \right ] \rp.
}
$$

### $G$-invariant functions on $T^*G$ and the Lie-Poisson bracket 

$f, h \in C^\infty(\fg^*)\ {}$ determine functions $\tilde f, \tilde h$ on $\ G \times \fg^*\ {}$ with trivial $G$ dependence, satisfying
$$\eqa{
 \fd {\tilde f} g(g, \mu)(\xi) + \nu(\fd {\tilde f} \mu(g, \mu)) &=  d \tilde f(g, \mu)(\xi_\lozenge(g), \nu) \\
&= df(\mu)(x\nu) \\
&= \nu(\fd f \mu(\mu)),
}
$$
and hence
$$
\{\tilde f, \tilde h \}_\lozenge(g, \mu) = \{f, h\}_\pm(\mu).
$$
The IVP 
$$
(\dot g, \dot \mu) = X_{\tilde h}(g, \mu) = \lp \lp \fd f \mu(\mu) \rp_{\!\lozenge}(g), \pm \ad^*_{\fd f \mu(\mu)} \mu \rp,
$$
with initial data $g(0) = g_0, \ \mu(0) = \mu_0, \ {}$ is partially decoupled. 

---

The solution $\mu: I \to \fg^*$ of the Euler equation
$$
\dot  \mu = \pm \ad^*_{\fd f \mu(\mu)} \mu \sands \mu(0) = \mu_0,
$$
determines a time-dependent IVP
$$
\dot g = d_1 \lozenge_g(\fd f \mu(\mu(t))) \sands g(0) = g_0.
$$
$~$
Finding $\, g(t)\, {}$ given $\, \mu(t)\, {}$ is one version of a process called *reconstruction*.

If $G \subseteq GL(n, F), \ {}$ the ODE for $g$ can be regarded as a linear ODE on $\, F^{n \times n}$.
$~$
If $\mu_0$ is an equilibrium of the Euler equation, i.e. $\ \ad^*_{\fd f \mu(\mu)} \mu = 0, \ {}$ then
$$
g(t) = \lozenge_{g_0}(\exp(t \, \xi)), \qquad \text{where} \quad \xi = \fd f \mu(\mu_0),
$$
and $g_0$ is called a *relative equilibrium*.

### Geodesics

A *Riemannian manifold* is a smooth manifold $M$ for which each tangent fiber $T_p M$ is equipped with an inner product $\langle \ \, , \ \rangle_p, \ {}$ and the inner products vary smoothly, in the sense that for any $X, Y \in \cXM, \ {}$
$$
p \mapsto \langle X(p), Y(p) \rangle_p \qquad \quad \text{is smooth.}
$$

Given a curve $\gamma: I \to M$, 
$$
\text{length} \, \gamma = \int_I |\gamma'(t)|_{\gamma(t)} dt.
$$

The calculus of variations yields a second order ODE, called the *geodesic equation* for $\gamma$. On a sufficiently small scale, the solutions are the shortest paths between their endpoints. 

These solutions are called *geodesics*.

Skipping over all of the variational machinery, if $(g, \mu): I \to G \times \fg^*$ is a solution of the "full version" of the Euler equations, then $g: I \to G$ satisfies the geodesic equation.

### Momentum maps 

Assume that a Lie group $G$ acts on the symplectic manifold $M$ and preserves the symplectic structure, i.e.
$$
\rho(g)^* \omega = \omega \qquad \qquad \forall \ g \in G.
$$
Given a map $J: M \to \fg^* \ {}$ and $\xi \in \fg, \ {}$ define $J_\xi \in C^\infty(M)\, {}$ by
$$
J_\xi(p) := J(p)(\xi).
$$

$J$ is a *momentum map* for the $G$ action on $M$ if
$$
X_{J_\xi} = \xi_M \qquad \qquad \forall \ \xi \in \fg.
$$
$~$
***Claim:*** If $h \in C^\infty(M)\, {}$ is $G$-invariant, then $J$ is preserved by the flow of $X_h$.

*Verify:* 
$$
X_h(J_\xi) = \omega(X_{J_\xi}, X_h) 
= - \omega(X_h, X_{J_\xi}) 
= - \xi_M(h) = 0.
$$

---

*Very important example:* $M = T^*Q, \ {}$ where $G$ acts on $Q$, and the action of $G$ on $T^*Q$ is 
$$
P(g)(z_q) = (d_{g \cdot q}\rho(g^{-1})^* z_q.
$$
***Claim:*** $\theta_0$ and $\omega_0$ are $G$-invariant, and 
$$
J_\xi(z_q) := z_q(\xi_Q(q)) 
$$
determines an equivariant momentum map $J$ on $T^*Q$, i.e.
$$
J(g \cdot z_q) = \Ad^*_{g^{-1}} J(z_q).
$$

*Verify:* $\ \pi \circ P(g) = \rho(g) \circ \pi \ \Longrightarrow$

$$\eqa{
(P(g)^*\theta_0)(z_q)(w_{z_q}) 
&= (g  \cdot z_q)(d_{g  \cdot z_q} \pi( d_{z_q} P(g)(w_{z_q}))\\
&= (g \cdot z_q)( d_{z_q} (\pi \circ P(g))(w_{z_q})\\
&= (d_{g \cdot q}\rho(g^{-1}))^* z_q(d_{z_q}(\rho(g) \circ \pi)(w_{z_q}))\\
&= z_q(d_{z_q} \pi(w_{z_q}))\\
&= \theta_0(z_q)(w_{z_q}).
}
$$

---

Hence $\theta_0$ is $G$-invariant.

$$\eqa{
P(g)^*\omega_0 &= - P(g)^*(\ed \theta_0) \\
&= - \ed (P(g)^*\theta_0) \\
&= - \ed \theta_0 \\
&= \omega_0
}
$$
$\Longrightarrow \ \omega_0$ is $G$-invariant, too.
$~$
$$
d_{z_q} \pi( \xi_{T^*Q}(z_q)) = \xi_Q(q) \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad 
J_\xi = \iota_{\xi_{T^*Q}} \theta_0,
$$
and hence 
$$\eqa{
d J_\xi &= d \iota_{\xi_{T^*Q}} \theta_0 \\
&= {\cal L}_{\xi_{T^*Q}} \theta_0 - \iota_{\xi_{T^*Q}} \ed \theta_0\\
&= \iota_{\xi_{T^*Q}} \omega_0,
}
$$
so $J$ is a momentum map.

---

***Claim:*** The momentum map $J: T^*Q \to \fg^*\ {}$ described above is $G$-equivariant, i.e.
$$
J(g \cdot z_q) = \Ad_{g^{-1}}^* J(z_q) \qquad \qquad \forall \ g \in G, \ z_q \in T^*Q.
$$

*Verify:*
$$\eqa{
J(g \cdot z_q)(\xi) &= J_\xi(g \cdot z_q) \\
&= (g \cdot z_q)(\xi_Q(\pi((g \cdot z_q))))\\
&= z_q (d_{g \cdot q}\rho(g^{-1})(\xi_Q(g \cdot q)))\\
&= z_q (\underbrace{\rho(g)^*\xi_Q}_{(\Ad_{g^{-1}} \xi)_Q}(q))\\
&= J_{\Ad_{g^{-1}} \xi}(z_q) \\
&= \Ad_{g^{-1}}^* J(z_q)(\xi)
}
$$
$\Longrightarrow \ J$ is $G$-equivariant.
$~$
*Consequence:* $G$-orbits in $T^*Q$ are mapped onto coadjoint orbits in $\fg^*$.
